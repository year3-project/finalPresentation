What the first prototype got wrong

Our Semester-1 prototype proved the concept — but living with it exposed five weaknesses, each now a hard requirement for this build.

G1
High latency after card tap
No real-time feedback at the cabinet.
G2
Insufficient storage
Too small for the range of tools needed.
G3
Poor RFID isolation
Tools just outside were still detected.
G4
No industrial design
An off-the-shelf box clashed with 4223.
G5
Weak lock mechanism
Too flimsy for daily, high-volume use.
→
Five problems, five goals

SAVE4223Protocols · Market & Design Goals
RFID Smart Inventory Cabinet09
Market Research → Design Goals

Two paths to manufacture, one set of goals

Surveying the market showed a clear split: build a bespoke prototype to prove the concept, or adopt a stock sheet-metal cabinet for scale. Both feed the same requirements.

01 Functional goals
  • Sturdy and large enough for varied tools, up to a hand drill
  • Mechanical lock that opens even during a power outage
  • RFID-enabled and properly isolated against misreads
  • Embeds all hardware — reader, locks, LED strip, QR/NFC, display
02 Industrial design goals
  • Match the existing 4223 furniture and maker-space style
  • Recess the display and QR reader so the top stays usable
  • Clean, clear UI with both Chinese and English support
  • Modern functionality without visual clutter
Prototype route
Aluminium-profile frame + composite panels — full control to DIY and validate.
Mass-production route
Stock sheet-metal cabinet — low cost, easy assembly, built-in RF shielding.

SAVE4223Architecture · Materials
RFID Smart Inventory Cabinet13
Materials · An Architecture of Compromise

Every panel does one job well

The enclosure must block RF; the drawers must pass it. We selected each material for a single property — shielding, transparency, stiffness, or strength.

ENCLOSURE · ISOLATE
Aluminium composite panel

A built-in Faraday cage — light, low-cost, V-groove folds to crisp corners.

DRAWER SIDES · PASS
PC hollow sunboard

Light and semi-transparent so UHF waves pass through; ribbed for stiffness.

DRAWER BASE · BEAR
Plywood

The hardest, load-bearing surface — and the safe touch surface.

FRAME · CARRY
Aluminium T-slot extrusion

Holds 200 kg and expands with pegboards and accessories.

Shielding physics · the core conflict

Target: 100% read inside, 0% misread outside. At 915 MHz the skin depth sets the shield: aluminium needs only ~0.15 mm, steel 304 ~0.78 mm. With wavelength λ ≈ 33 cm, any seam wider than λ/30 leaks — so conductive tape bridges every panel join.

Upgrade & scale

Honeycomb aluminium for the best stiffness-to-weight, and a stock steel filing cabinet at scale — harder, cheaper, with shielding built in.
